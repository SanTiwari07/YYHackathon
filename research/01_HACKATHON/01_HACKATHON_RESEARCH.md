## 1. Hackathon Overview & Brand Identity

The **Yuva Yodha Energy Tech Hackathon** is the flagship national innovation competition organized by **Schneider Electric India** in partnership with **YouNoodle** as the competition management platform.

The hackathon addresses real-world sustainability bottlenecks spanning agriculture, smart buildings, power distribution grids, and manufacturing decarbonization.

## 8. Official Judging Process & Evaluation Criteria

* `[FACT]` **First Evaluation Phase Scoring Breakdown (Total: 100%):**

| Evaluation Pillar | Weight | Official Criterion Description |
|---|:---:|---|
| **Impact & Measurability** | **25%** | Size and credibility of the quantified benefit against a clearly stated baseline and assumptions. |
| **Problem Understanding & Idea Quality** | **20%** | Clarity of problem framing and depth and originality of the idea, grounded in Indian realities. |
| **Architecture & Design** | **20%** | Soundness and clarity of the system architecture, design artefacts, and how well components fit together. |
| **Feasibility & Affordability** | **20%** | Practicality, cost, and ease of real-world deployment within Indian capacity and budget constraints. |
| **Sustainability** | **15%** | Broader energy, water, and emissions sustainability of the approach. |

* `[INTERPRETATION]` **Strategic Implications of the Weighting:**
  1. *Impact & Measurability is the highest single category (25%).* Hand-waving claims like "improves crop yield" or "saves lots of energy" will be heavily penalized. The proposal must present mathematically rigorous formulas, explicit baseline data sources (e.g., CEA, CGWB, ICAR), and measurable units (litres saved/ha, kWh displaced/pump, avoided CO₂e).
  2. *Feasibility & Affordability (20%) directly counteracts high-tech fantasy.* A ₹50,000 sensor setup on a 1-hectare farm will score near zero on feasibility. Solutions must demonstrate realistic unit economics for smallholder farmers earning ₹10,000–₹15,000/month.
  3. *Problem Understanding (20%) demands grounding in Indian realities.* The judges will scrutinize whether the team understands erratic 3-phase rural power supply, groundwater overexploitation, and free power subsidies.

## 10. Submission Requirements Breakdown

### Explicitly Required Deliverables:
1. **Detailed Solution Write-Up:** Explaining the mechanism of action, key assumptions, and why it is uniquely suited to Indian smallholder conditions.
2. **System / Architecture Diagram:** Clear visualization illustrating sensors, edge processing, software layers, and the complete flows of **data, energy, and money**.
3. **Supporting Design Artifacts:** UX dashboards, farmer mobile interface wireframes, sensor/data-model schematics, or service blueprints.
4. **Quantified Benefit Model:** Explicit mathematical model detailing water/energy savings, post-harvest loss mitigation, or yield uplift against a cited Indian baseline.
5. **Deployment & Scale-Up Plan:** Target crops, agro-climatic zones, farmer profiles, unit economics, and roll-out pathway.

### Hardware vs. Prototype Clarification:
* `[FACT]` **No Physical Hardware Build Expected:** The portal explicitly clarifies: *"A software prototype or simulation is encouraged, but not mandatory. No physical hardware build is expected."*
* `[INTERPRETATION]` While physical hardware fabrication is not mandatory, presenting a functioning software prototype with an interactive digital twin or physical simulation (e.g., simulated solar PV curve + pump motor dynamics + soil moisture physics) will dramatically outperform static slide decks during Phase 2 judging.
