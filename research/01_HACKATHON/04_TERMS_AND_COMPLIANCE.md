# Terms, Legal Compliance & Intellectual Property Guide

**Reference:** Official Rules of Yuva Yodha Energy Tech Hackathon 2026  
**Document ID:** HACK-LEGAL-04

## 1. Intellectual Property (IP) Rights & Boundaries

### Participant Ownership (Section 1)
* **Clause Summary:** The participant/team retains full and unencumbered ownership of all code, software repositories, mockups, algorithms, prototypes, and written documentation submitted to the hackathon.
* **No IP Forfeiture:** Submitting to Yuva Yodha does **not** transfer copyright, patent rights, or commercial ownership to Schneider Electric or YouNoodle.

### License Granted to Schneider Electric (Section 1)
* **Permitted Scope:** By submitting, participants grant Schneider Electric and its affiliates a worldwide, non-exclusive, royalty-free, perpetual license strictly for:
  1. Evaluating team performance during judging rounds;
  2. Marketing and promoting the competition in public media;
  3. Conducting recruitment assessments and Pre-Placement Interviews (PPI).
* **Commercialization Boundary:** If Schneider Electric wishes to incorporate the solution into commercial EcoStruxure™ products or license proprietary algorithms, a separate commercial licensing agreement or venture funding agreement (via SE Ventures) must be negotiated with the creators.

## 2. AI Usage & Originality Warranties

### Originality Clause (Section 1.3)
* Participants explicitly warrant that the submission consists of their own original technical work and does not infringe upon any third-party intellectual property, open-source licenses, or patents.

### Synthetic & AI-Generated Content Restrictions
* **The Rule:** *"You agree not to submit code containing unauthorized AI-generated content or plagiarized material."*
* **Compliance Protocol:**
  - AI tools (e.g., GitHub Copilot, Gemini) may be used for code assistance, refactoring, and documentation drafting.
  - However, the system architecture, mathematical equations, domain logic, and prototype mechanics must be rigorously understood, verified, and defensible by team members.
  - Submitting unverified "hallucinated" AI code that fails during live demonstration or contains unvetted open-source code with restrictive copyleft licenses (such as GPLv3) poses severe disqualification risk.
